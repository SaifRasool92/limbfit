Transradial Prosthetic Socket Design Guidelines
================================================

1. Introduction
---------------
A transradial prosthesis is a below-elbow artificial limb fitted to the residual limb after amputation. The socket is the most critical interface component. It must fit precisely to distribute load, prevent skin breakdown, and maintain suspension. Poor fit causes pain, skin ulcers, and rejection of the prosthesis.

2. Socket Wall Thickness
-------------------------
The recommended wall thickness for a hand-laminated thermoplastic or 3D-printed socket is 3.5 mm to 6.0 mm depending on the material.

- PLA (Polylactic Acid): Use 4.0–5.0 mm walls with 3–4 perimeters in slicer settings. PLA is rigid and lightweight but brittle under impact. Not recommended for active users.
- PETG (Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol): Use 3.5–4.5 mm walls. PETG is tougher and more flexible than PLA, with better layer adhesion. Preferred for rural clinic use.
- TPU (Thermoplastic Polyurethane): Can be used as a flexible liner insert at 2.0–3.0 mm. Used for soft interface layers, not structural walls.

All geometry measurements are in millimetres (mm). Never design a socket in inches.

3. Socket Relief and Fit
--------------------------
Relief refers to a deliberate enlargement of the socket lumen relative to the measured limb circumference. A relief of 0–3% is standard:

- 0% relief: Tight, total-contact socket. Used for well-matured residual limbs. Maximizes proprioception.
- 2% relief: General-purpose starting point for new patients. Allows for minor volume fluctuations during the day.
- 3–5% relief: Use when significant post-amputation edema is present or when the patient has fragile skin (diabetes, vascular disease).

Excessive relief (>5%) causes pistoning and skin shear. Insufficient relief (<0%) causes pressure sores over bony prominences.

4. Suspension Mechanisms
--------------------------
Common suspension strategies for transradial sockets:

- Self-suspension (anatomical): The socket is shaped to lock over the humeral epicondyles. No additional hardware needed. Best for active users in dusty environments.
- Locking liner: A silicone liner with a pin or lanyard mechanism. Highly effective but the liner must be replaced every 12–18 months.
- Supracondylar cuff: A leather or neoprene strap. Low cost and adjustable. Suitable for rural and low-resource settings.

5. Pressure-Sensitive Areas
-----------------------------
The following anatomical landmarks are bony prominences requiring relief in the socket design:

- Distal ulna and radius: Relieve 2–4 mm circumferentially at the distal end.
- Ulnar head: A small bubble relief of 5 mm radius is standard.
- Anterior distal radius: Avoid sharp edges in the socket trim line here.

Failure to relieve these areas causes chronic contact dermatitis and skin breakdown within 2–4 weeks of use.

6. Trim Lines
--------------
Trim lines define the proximal and distal edges of the socket.

- Distal trim line: Typically 10 mm proximal to the end of the residual limb to avoid end-bearing on the fragile stump tip.
- Proximal trim line: For short residual limbs (<40% of full forearm length), the socket must extend above the elbow joint (supracondylar extension) for better suspension and rotational stability.
- Anterior trim line: Should allow full elbow flexion to at least 135 degrees.

7. Limb Volume Fluctuation
----------------------------
Residual limb volume changes by 3–7% throughout the day due to activity and fluid shifts. Design considerations:

- Use a flexible inner socket (flexible thermoplastic or TPU) inside a rigid frame to accommodate volume change.
- Provide the patient with 3 mm and 6 mm prosthetic socks to add over the liner when volume decreases.
- Schedule re-evaluation at 3 months and 6 months post-fitting. Most volume stabilization occurs within 12 months post-amputation.

8. 3D Printing Settings for Sockets
--------------------------------------
Recommended FDM print settings for PETG prosthetic sockets:

- Layer height: 0.2 mm for structural parts, 0.15 mm for areas with fine anatomical detail.
- Infill: 40–60% gyroid or honeycomb infill. Avoids stress concentrations from rectilinear patterns.
- Perimeters/walls: 4 perimeters minimum for structural integrity.
- Print temperature: 235–245°C for PETG. Bed temperature: 70–80°C with PEI or glass bed.
- Cooling: Minimal cooling (20–30% fan) for PETG to avoid delamination between layers.
- Post-processing: Sand all inner socket surfaces to 400 grit. Smooth edges with a heat gun carefully.

Layer delamination is the primary failure mode for 3D printed sockets. Inspect all sockets before fitting.

9. Fit Assessment Protocol
----------------------------
The prosthetist must perform the following checks before issuing a socket:

Step 1 – Initial Donning:
  - Patient should be able to don the socket independently (with liner) in under 2 minutes.
  - Verify suspension: with socket donned, have patient hold arm downward. Socket should not slide off.

Step 2 – Skin Check:
  - Remove socket after 20 minutes of use. Inspect all skin.
  - Redness that blanches (hyperemia) within 10 minutes is acceptable.
  - Redness that persists >20 minutes or any blistering requires socket modification.

Step 3 – Functional Check:
  - Elbow flexion: patient should achieve at least 120 degrees of elbow flexion with socket in place.
  - Pronation/Supination: no restriction.
  - Confirm terminal device (hook or hand) operates correctly under load.

10. Materials Procurement in Rural Settings (Punjab)
------------------------------------------------------
Locally available materials suitable for socket fabrication:

- PETG filament (1.75 mm diameter) is available from Lahore and Karachi markets. Brands: Esun, Creality. Estimated cost: PKR 2500–4000 per kg.
- Polypropylene sheet (4 mm): Used for check sockets and thermoformed alternatives. Available from orthopaedic suppliers in Lahore.
- Silicone liner equivalents: TEC, Ossur, and local manufacturers in Lahore supply basic locking liners. Cost: PKR 8,000–15,000.

A single 3D-printed PETG socket requires approximately 200–350 grams of filament, costing PKR 500–1,400 per socket in materials — significantly cheaper than imported prefabricated options.

11. Safety and Clinical Disclaimers
-------------------------------------
All socket designs generated by automated tools are preliminary drafts only. They must be reviewed and approved by a qualified prosthetist or orthopaedic technician before patient use. This system is NOT a certified medical device and does not replace clinical judgment.

Risk factors requiring specialist referral:
  - Diabetes with peripheral neuropathy
  - Active wound or skin breakdown on residual limb
  - Congenital limb difference (different tissue properties)
  - Pediatric patients (require frequent re-fitting due to growth)
