3D Printing for Prosthetics — Technical Reference
===================================================

1. Printer Selection for Clinic Use
-------------------------------------
For a rural prosthetics clinic, a desktop FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) printer is the most practical and cost-effective choice. Recommended printers with reliable spare parts availability in Pakistan:

- Creality Ender 3 (or Pro/V2/S1): Widely available, large print community, spare parts in Lahore and Karachi. Build volume: 220x220x250 mm. Sufficient for most transradial sockets.
- Bambu Lab A1 Mini: Faster print speed, good reliability but higher initial cost.
- Prusa MK4: Excellent quality but may be difficult to source parts locally.

Minimum build volume for a transradial socket: 180 mm diameter x 150 mm height. Most Ender 3 variants meet this requirement.

2. Slicing Software
--------------------
Use free slicing software to prepare STL files for printing:

- Ultimaker Cura (recommended): Free, supports Creality printers, good profile management.
- Bambu Studio: For Bambu printers.
- PrusaSlicer: Excellent quality, recommended for clinical use.

Key slicer settings for prosthetic sockets:
  - Enable "Fuzzy Skin" on the outer surface for grip texture (optional).
  - Use "Variable Layer Height" — finer layers (0.1 mm) at the proximal brim for anatomical accuracy.
  - Enable "Ironing" on the top surface for a smooth trim line finish.
  - Set "Z-seam Alignment" to "Rear" to keep the seam on the posterior surface (less visible).

3. Print Orientation
---------------------
Socket orientation on the print bed is critical for structural strength:

CORRECT: Print the socket upright (distal end facing up, proximal opening on the bed). This aligns the layer lines perpendicular to the compressive loads during use.

INCORRECT: Printing the socket on its side creates layers parallel to the socket wall, causing catastrophic delamination under load.

Always inspect the first layer adhesion before leaving the printer unattended. A failed first layer wastes 8–12 hours of print time.

4. Support Structures
----------------------
Transradial sockets have complex interior geometry that may require supports:

- Use "Tree Supports" at 45-degree angle threshold.
- Support interface distance: 0.2 mm for easy removal without damaging socket interior.
- After printing, remove all support material from the interior of the socket using pliers and a heat gun.
- Sand all areas where supports contacted the socket surface.

5. Material Safety and Biocompatibility
-----------------------------------------
Not all 3D printing filaments are safe for prolonged skin contact:

- PETG: Considered skin-safe for external prosthetic applications when printed cleanly. Approved for use as socket material.
- PLA: Generally safe but contains additives that may cause irritation in some individuals. Avoid if patient has known polymer sensitivity.
- ABS: Not recommended. Releases styrene vapors during printing. Poor layer adhesion and warping in humid environments like Punjab.
- TPU (flexible): Safe for liner applications. Shore hardness 95A is standard for prosthetic liners.

Always print in a ventilated space. Install a HEPA filter enclosure on the printer if available.

6. Post-Processing Steps
--------------------------
After printing a socket, follow these steps before fitting:

Step 1 – Inspection:
  - Check all layer lines for delamination (visible gaps between layers).
  - Use a strong flashlight held at a tangent angle to the surface to reveal defects.
  - Reject any socket with visible delamination. Do not attempt to repair with glue.

Step 2 – Sanding:
  - Inner surface: Sand with 120 grit (rough), then 220 grit, then 400 grit.
  - Outer surface: Sand with 220 grit only for cosmetic finish.
  - Wear N95 mask during sanding to avoid inhaling fine plastic dust.

Step 3 – Edge Finishing:
  - Trim proximal and distal edges with a Dremel rotary tool or sharp knife.
  - Smooth all edges with a heat gun at low setting to remove sharp burrs.
  - A sharp socket edge will lacerate skin. Do not skip this step.

Step 4 – Test Fitting:
  - Check socket on a solid model or foam replica of the residual limb (same circumference) before patient fitting.
  - Socket should slide onto model with moderate hand pressure but not fall off under gravity.

7. Failure Modes and Troubleshooting
---------------------------------------
Common 3D-printed socket failures and their causes:

| Failure | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Layer delamination | Low print temperature, high cooling, wet filament | Dry filament 4h at 65°C, increase temp by 5°C |
| Warping | Poor bed adhesion | Use PEI bed, brim, bed at 80°C |
| Stringing | Too high print temperature | Reduce by 5°C, enable retraction |
| Under-extrusion | Partial nozzle clog | Cold pull cleaning |
| Socket cracks at trim line | Stress concentration, too thin wall | Increase perimeters, add fillet at trim line |

8. Filament Storage
--------------------
PETG absorbs moisture from humid air (Punjab's humidity can reach 80%+). Moist filament causes:
  - Bubbling and popping during printing
  - Poor layer adhesion
  - Rough surface finish
  - Reduced tensile strength

Store filament in sealed containers with silica gel desiccant. Before using a new roll, dry PETG in an oven at 65°C for 4 hours. Do not exceed 70°C or the filament will soften in the tube.

9. Maintenance Schedule for Clinic Printer
--------------------------------------------
A prosthetics clinic printer must be maintained regularly to ensure dimensional accuracy:

- Weekly: Level the print bed using a 0.1 mm feeler gauge or auto-leveling routine.
- Monthly: Clean the nozzle with a cold pull (nylon filament at 90°C). Lubricate Z-axis lead screw with PTFE grease.
- Quarterly: Replace the nozzle (brass nozzle wears with abrasive filaments). Check belt tension.
- Annually: Replace the print bed surface and Bowden tube (if applicable).

Dimensional accuracy requirement for prosthetic sockets: ±0.5 mm. Validate by printing a 50x50x10 mm calibration cube before each socket print.
